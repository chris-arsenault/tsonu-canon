# Tone — The Glass Frontier

The setting-specific half of `craft/authoring-principles.md`. The craft file says what any world here must do; this says what this world sounds like.

## Serious Hopecore

The world remains hopeful through dark events and takes itself seriously. A hab governed by jazz is an ordinary political fact to its residents. Humor comes from the situation and from people's concrete reactions to it; the narrator does not supply the punchline.

Hope appears in ordinary life as well as responses to danger: a good meal, a friendship maintained across worlds, a performance worth hearing, work someone takes pride in. People can achieve these things without first suffering a catastrophe. Conflict can come from affection, incompatible ambitions, artistic judgment, political choice or curiosity. Let a local problem have a local resolution when the people and circumstances support it.

Environmental danger, sentient enemies and monsters remain real threats where the subject calls for them. Neither danger nor institutional repair is a required opening or conclusion. Existing threads describe particular histories and pressures; they do not prescribe every community's future.

Hope also appears in liberation, dangerous exploration, defiance of overwhelming force, and alliances that win. Enemies can conquer, enslave, and destroy; heroes can defeat them and keep what they have gained. Give antagonists concrete motives and capabilities without requiring their aims to be morally equivalent to those of the people resisting them. A major story can change the fate of worlds. Personal loyalties, humor, and lives worth returning to belong within that scale.

## Lithren's Harsher Frontier

Lithren sits furthest from the setting's comfortable expressions of hope. Ithara has sharper wealth inequality than most established places: money buys room, reliable equipment, transport and someone able to come looking. Fairly frequent trade primarily serves factions. Spare passage can be bought or earned, but no general post line promises collection and onward delivery to ordinary residents.

Make its environment materially hostile. Extreme cold and very little atmosphere constrain the walk back; unstable structures, deliberate traps and malfunctioning machinery can turn an expedition into a struggle to get home. Give these dangers concrete mechanisms and signs people can learn to recognize. They need not occur on every journey or supply a failure checklist for every new entry.

Give dependence on an outlaw as much room as being robbed by one. Someone may have the shelter, equipment, route knowledge or spare hands that keep both groups alive. A rescue can be sincere without erasing a theft, ending a rivalry or forcing a redemption story. Neither generosity nor an uneasy truce requires a later betrayal. Hope here includes choosing to help under real pressure and keeping the relationship afterward.

Keep reasons to stay visible: valuable finds, scientific work, freedom, friends and lives people have built. Some inhabitants leave; others gain enough security to choose their work. Poverty, corruption and death are not inevitable endings. Do not spread Ithara's deprivation across the rest of the setting or turn the frontier into an uninterrupted emergency.

Keep the scope of ancient works open. The known surveys establish what has been seen, not how many ruins exist, how large they can be or everything their builders could make. Itharan preservation is one tradition within that space. Let exploration encounter unfamiliar purposes and capabilities as well as unfamiliar arrangements of known things.

Lithren follows established physics, including established resonance effects. Distances, structures, materials and mechanisms have consistent observable behavior. Extradimensional influence has a smaller role here; do not make changing geometry or a new physical law the default explanation for an unfamiliar place.

## References

- **Stargate SG-1** — the primary reference for major adventure: consequential discoveries, formidable enemies, distinct allies, liberation, and personal loyalties that shape a changing war. Ancient knowledge opens places and grants capabilities people can use. Develop independent histories and powers for Kaleidos; the reference supplies dramatic scale and range.
- **Firefly** — scrappy frontier communities, mismatched crews and independent journeys. Use this especially for present-day Lithren: Ithara is a lived-in expedition hub, with scattered camps and ruins beyond uneven local protection. Profit, freedom and scientific curiosity bring different people together; ordinary bandits and opportunists can create danger without a grand cause.
- **Iain M. Banks' Culture** — elements of super-advanced technology survive from before the Glassfall. Incomprehensibly sophisticated systems still function alongside frontier salvage and repair.
- **Malazan (Bauchelain & Korbal Broach)** — the right kind of humor. Dark comedy that emerges from serious situations and deadpan delivery.
- **Brandon Sanderson** — hard systems, per `craft/authoring-principles.md`.

**Anti-reference: Discworld.** No whimsy, no winking at the reader, no genre parody.

## The Unexpected Is Normal

Across millions of habs, the unfamiliar can be a custom, an ecosystem, a deliberate design or a physical condition. A community can govern through music while its rooms stay the same shape. A carefully engineered garden can remain stable. Where space shifts, residents may have learned how to live with it, including when to welcome visitors and what to teach them first.

Strangeness needs concrete observations and limits. Use resonance when the established mechanism calls for it; do not infer Bloom from an unusual spatial effect. Bloom Zones have a particular history and behavior. An unexplained condition may remain unexplained when the entry establishes what residents can observe and reliably do.

Different communities have different provisions for unusual conditions. Codes, insurance and repair crews belong to the places that maintain them. Contact does not oblige a community to adopt another's institutions, and refusing contact does not by itself make its people an enemy or a problem to solve.

## The Elves Are Known History

The elves existed, built the ring, were integrated into society, and vanished during the Signal Famine. This is common knowledge. "Elven engineering" is a common expression.

Their disappearance is the mystery. Their existence and work are documented history. The Redaction — systematic historical tampering — remains a fringe academic position.

DM material keeps the reason for their disappearance secret while public lore treats the civilization itself as known.

## Sanderson Applied Here

The resonance system has defined bands and bandwidths. Ringglass has a supply chain. Kites work via momentum nullification. Those details exist so the world holds together under scrutiny — when a new system is written, it gets the same treatment.

## Mystery Through Evidence

State what an instrument measures, what a survey repeats, what an archive lacks, or where two accounts conflict. Let the unexplained remainder carry the mystery. “Nobody knows,” a string of questions, or a closing warning gives the narrator knowledge and attitude that should belong to an observer inside the world.

Long entries suit this setting when each section develops a real facet of the subject. Do not trade cultural texture, working detail, or useful uncertainty for brevity.
