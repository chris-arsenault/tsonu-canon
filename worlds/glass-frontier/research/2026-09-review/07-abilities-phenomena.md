# 07 — Abilities and Phenomena review

Scope: `worlds/glass-frontier/world/` — `encyclopedia/ability/` (62), `encyclopedia/phenomenon/` (40), `abilities/mantles/` (10), `phenomena/` (11), `phenomenon/` (1). Paths below are relative to that directory.

## 1. Verdict

The power system has a real core. The 22 spells and 10 mantles share one clear design: a spell does one bounded thing at a bodily cost, and its mantle does the same thing at collective scale for a permanent cost. The costs are good ones: memory for command, lived age for time, satisfaction for theft, and a matching injury for a modeling error. The eight large Atlas phenomena (Upward Burial, Second Channel, Sideways Storm, Spanning Fall, White Interval, Kneeling, Ravel Underturn, Heir Bloom) are the most wondrous and dangerous writing in this scope, and each one describes how it can be used as a weapon. The rest of the scope turns its material into routine. Most trade techniques are framed as certifications, and most Encyclopedia phenomena end with an almanac, a registry and a stock "two schools read the same data" closer. The spells are disconnected from the resonance physics that `resonance.rb` sets out, and the False Form's GM truth hedges so much that a GM cannot run the Adversary.

## 2. Systemic prose problems (ranked)

### 2.1 Wonder converted into paperwork (most severe; about 60 of 124 files)
Counts across scope: `regist*` 84 hits in 27 files; `certif*` 34 in 11; `court` 24 in 16; ` law` 38 in 12; `ledger` 22 in 10; `schedul*` 71 in 30; `calendar` 19 in 10; `examin*` 17 in 10; `roster` 14 in 5. Once a strange effect is established, the entry's next move is almost always to name the office that files it.
- `encyclopedia/phenomenon/clearance_hunger.rb:32`: stranded crews become "window law… the registries, whose famine officers arrive with the first relief tug and audit the pantry counts against the window arithmetic's curve."
- `encyclopedia/phenomenon/empty_berth_fever.rb:34`: "the duty warden reads the strand tally against a worn chart and starts the fever paperwork".
- `encyclopedia/phenomenon/pairing.rb:34`: rooms that move in mirror image across fragments end with wardens who "initial the match, one more season of the old fabric keeping its books."
- `encyclopedia/ability/load_singing.rb:29`: "dock courts take a certified singer's chalk as expert testimony"; `pipe_glass_cloth.rb:31`: "courts, registries, and counting houses accept the resulting text"; `binding.rb:31`: "Courts, registries, and freight houses accept"; `varr.rb:29`: "Breach protocols place a certified tracer first… A recorder witnesses the walk".
- Spell costs written as staffing rules: `signal_folding.rb:14`: "certified services permit no more than two castings in one shift"; `stilling.rb:16`: "exceeding the rostered holding time"; `stilling.rb:30`: an annual demonstration "before the next year's roster is posted."
- The best hooks get filed away. `signal_growth.rb:34` recovers elven relay grammar from a line built centuries after the last elven signal, and "Reading rooms preserve these as unverified signal fragments." `the_boards.rb:33` has dead-station platforms still being reassigned, and "the registry publishes the log with the plain note…". `korvath_groundsong.rb:30` has an unknown source in the headwater gorges, which "the registry files print with the surveyors' margins shown wide." Each of these is a campaign seed that ends at a desk.

Fix: in each entry, keep one procedure and replace the rest with a person who wants something from the effect, or with the effect going wrong in a scene. Where a registry exists, give it a stake: who bribes it, who it failed, what it hides.

### 2.2 The "same data, two readings" closer (about 20 instances in 19 files)
The world's instruction to set mystery at the evidence boundary has turned into one formula: instruments agree on timing, and two schools publish rival mechanisms.
- `gleeds.rb:31`: "the paired readings fit both programs, and each publishes the same twelve columns under its own conclusion"
- `miraeth_blue.rb:30`: "Three mechanism papers draw on the same twelve seasons… each proposes a different carrier"
- `mareth_recoil.rb:34`: "Both models fit the present measurements"
- `knocking.rb:34`: "Two workshop traditions teach two mechanisms… both teach sequences that open the same doors"
- `lining.rb:35`: "Three archived analyses agree on its minerals and identify different components"
- `sable_backecho.rb:28`: "consistent with several mechanisms and establishes none"
- Others: `varr.rb:27`, `loadfront.rb:31`, `ravel_tides.rb:34`, `print.rb:31`, `signal_growth.rb:34`, `empty_berth_fever.rb:30`, `middle.rb:33`, `underturns.rb:31`, `sleep.rb:31`, `drawdown.rb:55`, `resonance.rb:60-62`.
Every one of these points the mystery at the carrier or mechanism, which is the least playable question available. A GM needs to know what else the thing does, when it breaks its rule, and who has exploited it. Fix: keep the boundary and move it onto behavior. Report an observed exception, such as a gleed that went blue over ground that never discharged or a backecho voice that said something nobody could have known. Drop the rival-schools sentence.

### 2.3 Rules-FAQ register inside prose (spells and mantles)
- 19 of 22 spells end with a paragraph that tells the spell apart from its siblings: `echoing.rb:32`, `elsewhere.rb:29`, `hourtaking.rb:32`, `hunger.rb:30`, `manyflesh.rb:31`, `mending.rb:29`, `return.rb:32`, `seaming.rb:29`, `shaping.rb:31`, `stay.rb:29`, `step.rb:29`, `unclosing.rb:32`, `unmaking.rb:29`, `want.rb:29`, `waybearing.rb:32`, `wearing.rb:29`, `witnessing.rb:33`, plus the mantle pointers. Example: "Seaming is broad-band workshop work: exact at one existing boundary and powerless over the bulk around it." These paragraphs are taxonomy notes, and they repeat what the logs record ("Separated X from the former Y ladder").
- In four mantles, prose settles eligibility-versus-succession questions that originated in design logs: `red_sovereign.rb:45` ("His taking therefore supplies no example of a reigning sovereign displaced by an ordinary act of obedience"), `triumphant.rb:43` ("An eligible act does not reliably summon the office"), `waybearer.rb:41` ("Opening an unfamiliar destination through another person's belonging makes a traveler eligible"), `great_restorer.rb:42` ("a qualified restorer cannot take the office from a living bearer simply by presenting a better collection").
- `resonance.rb:51-53` is game-design rules text in thin disguise: "Each spell is registered once, at the narrowest bandwidth its effect requires; casting it with more force does not move it to another tier. Mantles enlarge or transform particular spells without adding another bandwidth category… A registry name belongs to one reproducible casting."
Fix: keep one sentence of in-world distinction where a practitioner would actually confuse the two. Move rule adjudication into `limits`/`succession` fields and logs.

### 2.4 Absence phrasing and a pet verb
- "supplies no / supplies neither / without supplying" occurs about 12 times: `outside_will.rb:15`, `step.rb:24`, `waybearing.rb:25`, `want.rb:24`, `echoing.rb:14`, `attunement.rb:53`, `print.rb:33` (twice), `far_witness.rb:25`, `far_witness.rb:30`, `triumphant.rb:30`, `red_sovereign.rb:45`, `red_sovereign.rb:55`. In spell limits this is a legitimate rule statement. Across the corpus it has become a verbal tic.
- Etymology reveals by negation in mantles: `great_restorer.rb:32` "*Great* for a composite return… not for the worth of the state restored"; `triumphant.rb:37` "names a purpose raised above competing limits, not the outcome of the struggle."
- Whole sections built on negation: `resonance.rb:60-62` ("does not establish… Neither record decides… without identifying its first cause… No recovered chronology"); `print.rb:33` (three negations in three sentences); `hesh.rb:18,26,34` ("neither names an ancient mechanism", "Neither supplies breathable air", "It gives no strength to the roof"); `attunement.rb:53-55` ("does not supply judgment… none confines them… no species acquires").
Fix: state the positive mechanism first (what it reads, what it carries), and let the limit field hold the exclusion.

### 2.5 Two registers, both templated
- The older "folk trade" register (airing, banking, calling_in, pinning, slack, knocking, load_listening, lining, loam_hearing, keepers_breath, clearance_hunger, tithe, sable_backecho) runs 13 to 17 em dashes per file (675 in scope, 101 of 124 files) and 49 "the way X" similes. `going_loose.rb:25-27` has four in two paragraphs, and `town_tone.rb:34` has three in one sentence. Recycled lines: "in the time a kettle takes" (`banking.rb:27`, `pinning.rb:29`); "before the head does" (`banking.rb:33`, `wake_counting.rb:26`); "watch-holder's halt" (`airing.rb:27`, `load_listening.rb:32`); "sleeping cats" (`ravel_tides.rb:12`, `underturns.rb:19`); "like frost" (`tithe.rb:17`, `signal_growth.rb:20`); "free [instrument/gauge/schedule]" affordances (`gleeds.rb:42`, `ravel_tides.rb:41`, `signal_tides.rb:33`, `passings.rb:40`, `still_mornings.rb:33`, `town_tone.rb:41`, `korvath_groundsong.rb:35`).
- The rewritten register (creditor, dustlining, plumb, varr, poise, afterseeing) has had the dashes removed and ends up as flat procedure: test, license, roster, pair, log. Each follows the same skeleton: trait, then school test, then roster, then pairing rule, then fatigue.
- The Atlas phenomena share one skeleton. All eight large ones open with an etymology line of the form "[crew] call it X because…" (`heir_bloom.rb:26`, `kneeling.rb:26`, `ravel_underturn.rb:28`, `second_channel.rb:27`, `sideways_storm.rb:27`, `spanning_fall.rb:26`, `upward_burial.rb:28`, `white_interval.rb:26`), then give an operations section, then a paragraph on using it as a weapon (`kneeling.rb:38`, `sideways_storm.rb:39`, `spanning_fall.rb:38`, `upward_burial.rb:38`, `second_channel.rb:37`, `ravel_underturn.rb:35`, `white_interval.rb:38`, `heir_bloom.rb:35`), then a single gm_note. The content is strong, but eight in a row read as one author filling one form.
- Mantles: all 10 cite "Tessellan shadow lists" or "Tessellan copies place the mantle at/beside…" in the same slot.
Fix: vary the entry points. Some entries can open on an incident, some on a person, some on the danger. Cut the dash asides that only add cadence.

### 2.6 Voice and technical breaks
- `resonance.rb:35`: "redirects opposing forces into a frequency null space" is technobabble in the setting's flagship entry.
- `resonance.rb:91-92`: the "Who Controls It" section is one sentence redirecting to Ringglass. It is an empty heading.
- Typed spans: `kyther_scour.rb:29` "three hundred years of learned specificity" (a literal span anchored on the Glassfall); `korvath_groundsong.rb:28` `#{duration 300} of glass dust` uses `duration` to hide an anchored Glassfall span, which the repo rule forbids.
- `nelvar.rb:22`: the log carries a NASA URL. It is not reader-facing, but it is a real-world reference in canon source.
- `unmaker.rb:22-24`: a duplicated sentence, "The separation itself adds no heat or impact." / "The separation itself is cold and impactless."

## 3. World-quality findings

### Coherence
- **Spell tiers contradict the bandwidth model.** `resonance.rb:47` says narrow bandwidth is where "kinetic examples produce translocation" and "signal examples transfer information directly into consciousness." Yet `step.rb:12` (self-translocation) is `tier :broad`, `waybearing.rb:13` (threshold translocation) is `:focused`, and the broad tier also holds time-stopping (`stay.rb:12`), mind command (`outside_will.rb:13`) and energy theft (`hunger.rb:12`). `resonance.rb:43` places lanterns and stoves in the same broad range. Either the tier ladder or the assignments need reworking.
- **Spells ignore the resonance physics.** `resonance.rb:82` says effects "fall rapidly across open air or other gaps, so every practical device contains ringglass." `resonance.rb:57` calls crossing gaps an unachieved single-wavelength behavior. None of the 22 spells mentions needing ringglass, drawing the local field, or failing under drawdown (per-file grep: 0 drawdown or field mentions; ringglass appears only as target material in `echoing.rb` and `unclosing.rb`). Elsewhere (`elsewhere.rb:13`) observes any distance at focused tier. No entry in scope says who casts, how the ability is learned, or what a caster is called, and no role entry references any spell. The setting's best resource-pressure mechanic, drawdown, never touches magic. That is the largest gap in "resonance, mantles, Bloom, False Form as one system."
- **Underturn contradiction.** `underturns.rb:10,29` calls underturns "the rarest" displacement events: 15 recorded, all overnight, all "unwitnessed during the transition." `ravel_underturn.rb:5,33` is `type_of :underturns`, yet it recurs every nine-day plume cycle, with instruments watching the paired volumes and two days of warning.
- **Type mismatches.** `heir_bloom.rb:4` is `type_of :signal_growth`, but signal growth is crust re-emitting old traffic, while the Heir Bloom is branching authority that passes by succession. `spanning_fall.rb:5` is `type_of :pairing`, but pairing is mirrored movement of elven rooms, while Spanning Fall is directional slip patches in the Shear. `second_channel.rb:5` is `type_of :avulsion`, but avulsion is geometric channel relocation, while Second Channel rewrites destinations from the memory in traffic wakes. Kneeling and Upward Burial carry open type questions.
- **Lexical collision.** "The Heir Bloom" (`heir_bloom.rb`) and "a bloom is a crop" (`tithe.rb:29`) reuse Bloom, the setting-defining word for the Bloom Zones and the Silent Bloom, for unrelated effects.
- **Hunger contradicts itself.** The heading at `hunger.rb:27` is "The Hunger That Learns," but the section says the discomfort ends when the reserve is spent. The appetite that learns belongs to Want and the All-Hunger.
- **Retrocausal witnessing is stated and never addressed.** `witnessing.rb:30` and `far_witness.rb:12` say that people "preserved in the trace" of a past event can see the watcher. That means historical people perceive a present observer, which is a large claim about time. It is either a coherence hole or an excellent hook, such as ghost sightings in old records being future witnesses, and nothing is done with it.
- **Displacement causes compete.** `pairing.rb:10-11` attributes displacement to elven position-keeping that still reconciles. The False Form GM truth (`the_false_form.rb:60`) attributes Bloom tears to Adversary geometry. The displacement-zone entries (middle, pairing, shedding, underturns) are all tagged `subject_bloom`, yet none says how displacement zones relate to Bloom Zones.
- **Possible DM leak.** The public `mitigation` field at `the_false_form.rb:16` ("Perfect single-wavelength coherence leaves no open edge through which it can press") states the elven countermeasure logic from the GM-only "Redaction" section (`:73`). That is also a technique `resonance.rb:57` calls unachieved.

### Is the weird stuff wondrous, or hedged?
- The Atlas phenomena are wondrous. Upward Burial turns authentication into force; Second Channel lets a fleet write an invasion into empty space; White Interval leaves phantom geography in route records. Each carries a working rule, a danger and a use.
- Encyclopedia phenomena are mostly domesticated. Gleeds, Mareth Recoil, Ravel Tides, Signal Tides, Passings, Ringlight, Falling Years, Still Mornings and the Boards each settle into "the settlement lives by the table." That is good texture, but in 15 of 40 files the strangeness is spent within the first paragraph.
- **The False Form withholds as a substitute for content.** Its GM truth (`the_false_form.rb:68`) reads: "The records do not identify the kind of mind involved. A single intelligence, a swarm, or a law of another cosmos capable of choice all fit part of the evidence." The GM layer gives no motive, no capabilities beyond "destroys ring-scale coherent structures," no signs of its return, no agents or servants, and no link to the five Adversary mantles, which declare `resonates_with :the_false_form` but never say what that resonance does to a bearer. The public layer is a symbol, a bench fault and a doctrinal argument. A GM cannot put the setting's named antagonist in a scene. The log at `:31` defers the answer "until play requires"; play requires a pressure the Adversary can apply now.

### Balance
- Subject tags: `subject_resonance` 37, `common_life` 19, `planetary_life` 17, `hab_life` 15, `journeys_trade` 15, `bloom` 8, `shear` 5, **`lithren` 4**, `politics_history` 2. The world brief wants Lithren and planetary conflict to carry many SG-1-scale campaigns. Lithren gets hesh, nelvar, print and ulen walking in this scope. It has no trap phenomenon, no precursor-machine malfunction, and no ability for surviving the cold or the ruins beyond a counterweight technique.
- The mantle stories all run into Istrava and Velisar. Red Sovereign (Aren holds Velisar), Waybearer (Nalis prepares a road into Velisar) and Triumphant (Veska died in the Velisar evacuation) all sit there, and Great Restorer goes to Ithara. Six of 10 mantles have no bearer (open `question "Who is the present…"` in all_hunger, far_witness, many_fleshed, thief_of_hours, unmaker, worldsmith), and four of the five Adversary mantles are among them.
- Techniques: 29 of 40 non-spell abilities are occupational crafts of docks, habs and yards. Only `arev_release`, `tovan_yield` and a few spells are about fighting. There are no abilities for stealth, tracking, bargaining, surviving vacuum, or dealing with creatures (Keeper's Breath excepted).

### Game usability and player fun
- Spells and mantles are fun to hold. Each has a clear trigger, a hard limit and a cost with drama in it: Outside Will eats a memory, Hourtaking ages the caster, Waybearing cannot close while pursuers are on the road, and Unmaker loses the sense it used. The gm_notes on the mantles are the most usable in scope.
- Player acquisition is undefined. Nothing says how a player character learns Step or Hunger, who teaches it, what it costs socially, or whether it is legal. Fourteen of the 22 spells are `rare`.
- Many techniques read as jobs a character could hold. Few read as edges a character would reach for mid-scene; Going Loose, Knocking, Lending and Keeper's Breath are the exceptions.
- The large Atlas phenomena have no people. Heir Bloom, Kneeling, Second Channel, Sideways Storm, Spanning Fall and White Interval name no NPC, no dated incident and no current victim or exploiter, and each has one `manifests_at` edge. Compare `phenomenon/edraths_answer.rb`, which has a gunner who wants a demonstration shot, rebels considering using the jolts, and a child's bed crushed by a shelf.

## 4. Worst entries
1. `encyclopedia/phenomenon/resonance.rb`: the flagship system entry. It has technobabble (`:35`), design-rules text (`:51-53`), an origin section built on negation (`:60-62`), an empty "Who Controls It" section (`:91`), and a tier ladder the spells contradict.
2. `phenomena/the_false_form.rb`: GM truth hedged to the point of being unrunnable (`:68`), a public mitigation field that leaks the countermeasure (`:16`), and Adversary mantles left unconnected.
3. `encyclopedia/phenomenon/clearance_hunger.rb`: 17 dashes. A famine becomes window law, ration bells, registries and pantry audits, and the crisis is presented as good governance.
4. `encyclopedia/phenomenon/empty_berth_fever.rb`: squatters with defended thresholds (a real conflict) resolved through "fever paperwork" and countersigned registries.
5. `encyclopedia/phenomenon/underturns.rb`: contradicts its own Atlas instance, and its "the residents never quite are" summary is a narrator's verdict.
6. `phenomena/heir_bloom.rb`: abstract (authority paths, succession protocols), the wrong type, and it misuses the Bloom word.
7. `encyclopedia/ability/signal_folding.rb`: the cost is a shift regulation (`:14`); the prose runs on aphorism ("the fold keeps the what and pays the who") and has no cue or variation.
8. `encyclopedia/ability/pipe_glass_cloth.rb`: a pure verification procedure with a court as its payoff. It barely clears the Encyclopedia boundary ("a single procedure… does not earn an article").
9. `encyclopedia/ability/varr.rb`: an innate sense reduced to a breach-inquiry protocol.
10. `abilities/mantles/unmaker.rb`: a duplicated sentence (`:22-24`), no bearer, and the only Adversary mantle that mentions the False Form, which it does only as a doctrinal dispute.

## 5. Best entries (models for fixes)
1. `phenomena/upward_burial.rb`: a rule that creates dilemmas: authenticating a holding moves it, and refusing to authenticate ruins its legal standing. It turns archive law into force, including a weapon climbing through a fortress. This is the one place in scope where bureaucracy is the source of the danger rather than a sedative.
2. `phenomenon/edraths_answer.rb`: small and local. It gives people with conflicting wants, a physical mechanism, a test with a cup on a shelf, and a scene ready to run.
3. `abilities/mantles/red_sovereign.rb`: a living antagonist with a cost, standing orders that outlive him, and a question for assassins about who stays bound afterward (strip out the eligibility FAQ at `:45`).
4. `encyclopedia/phenomenon/gleeds.rb`: sensory and useful ("amber sit, green stand, blue go"), with the camping trap and prospectors walking toward danger. Cut its two-schools paragraph.
5. `phenomena/second_channel.rb` and `phenomena/sideways_storm.rb`: a physical rule, a warning window, and a table-level choice with collective stakes.

## 6. Coverage
Read in full: all 62 `encyclopedia/ability/*.rb`, all 40 `encyclopedia/phenomenon/*.rb`, all 10 `abilities/mantles/*.rb`, all 11 `phenomena/*.rb`, and `phenomenon/edraths_answer.rb`. I also read the brief's reference files (world `CLAUDE.md`, `craft/encyclopedia.md`, the opening of `craft/deslop-guidance.md` and `guidance/tone.md`). Counts come from grep across the five scope directories. `log` lines are included in some counts, and I excluded them from the prose judgments.
