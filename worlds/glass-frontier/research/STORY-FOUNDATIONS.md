# Glass Frontier: Three Narrative Foundations

Approved narrative treatments, encoded September 15, 2026. This document is editorial context; the linked DSL entries own canon. Execution state and validation are in [STORY-RANGE-PLAN.md](STORY-RANGE-PLAN.md#m1a--establish-narrative-foundations).

Retain Bloom's established people and causes; build the planetary conflict around a regional defense league captured by a bearer of the Red Sovereign; build Lithren around an ancient civilization whose repeated restorations preserved both extraordinary achievements and the means of its domination.

The foundations use existing powers. Aren, Nalis and Eris are attested current bearers; Veska, Soma and Eris's predecessor have dated earlier service. Lithren's recovered actions are public knowledge. Its ancient opponent, ecological causes, ultimate fate and possible role in originating resonance remain intentionally open for every audience, with no concealed answer.

| Area | Central struggle | What its history makes possible now |
|---|---|---|
| Bloom/Shear | Responsibility for a catastrophe and the lives built around its boundaries | Containment, recovery, dangerous exploration, inherited loyalties, and the existing hidden conflict |
| Planetary conflict | Whether people can free themselves from a power that once protected them | Occupation and resistance, contested alliances, rescues, military expeditions, defections, and succession |
| Lithren | Whether recovering a civilization requires repeating the choices that preserved it | Expeditions, powerful recoveries, encounters with the consequences of ancient people, competing restorations, and the discovery of a wider past |

Each area can sustain independent campaigns. Shared resonance and religious vocabulary do not establish a shared conspiracy or cause. Habs and ordinary life also remain available outside these conflicts.

## 1. Bloom and the Shear

### The foundation already present

**Existing canon.** Bloom containment grows from a legitimate desire for access. The reconnecting system wanted ringglass and communication beyond the few Span termini. Coremark's Second Span Trial failed in 2356; its abandoned guide chain later carried Pyre's 2378 overload through the mid-drift. The trial did not itself cause the refinery overload. That distinction keeps ambition, bad engineering decisions, and institutional culpability from collapsing into one accusation.

Lira's careful work existed before anyone called it containment. Crowncut's service records made her discoverable when larger schemes failed. Dern was evacuated from Latch as a child and later chose cordon work. Oram made the Council answerable to displaced families through its recovery records. Teren preserved evidence his employer wanted dismissed, yet refuses to help anyone build another Span. Their decisions leave different kinds of inheritance: a technology, an institution, a surviving accusation, a limit someone will not cross.

The useful thematic tension extends beyond guilt. Dern did not cause the Bloom. Neither did the children now growing up beside cordons. Responsibility can become meaningful work, coercion, identity, or a reason someone never allows themselves to leave. People who demand safer work and people who demand access to lost homes both have claims that the Council cannot satisfy simply by remaining neutral.

Sources: [Bloom Containment](../world/threads/bloom_containment.rb), [Second Span Trial](../world/history/events/second_span_trial.rb), [Lira](../world/npcs/heroes/lira_vashtenri.rb), [Dern](../world/npcs/heroes/dern_talish.rb), [Oram](../world/npcs/heroes/oram_sells.rb), [Teren](../world/npcs/heroes/teren_voss.rb), [Displacement Council](../world/npcs/factions/displacement_council.rb).

### Threads to develop

**Containment and its inheritance.** Keep the existing industrial and containment sequence. Bring its later changes into the thread: Oram's station handoffs, Dern's succession, the Council's remote recovery work, and Lira's attempt to retire saturated buoys. These are changes in what people can do and whom they must trust. The present question is whether successful containment can become work that others can learn, share, and survive. A working new method is allowed to remain a success.

**The lives beyond a closed boundary.** Give the diaspora's continuing life as much causal weight as its evacuation. Existing family requests at Karet and the Latch registry already establish that a boundary separates people from particular histories. Develop disagreements within those families: some want objects returned, some want access, some want their new homes to stop being treated as temporary. A retrieved object can settle a family matter without revealing the nature of the Bloom. A person who leaves the Council can remain loyal to those who stay.

**Access after the trial.** Teren's refusal does not extinguish the demand that financed his project. Preserve a distinction between another attempt at a Span, ordinary improvements to communication, and a challenge to who benefits from existing routes. A safe improvement can be worthwhile without solving the old inequality. This thread may touch planetary politics through a particular proposal, but its history does not become the cause of the new political movement.

The Shear also contains salvage, communities, working relationships, and anomalies outside Bloom containment. This treatment develops the established Bloom history within that wider geography; it does not make every Shear subject one of its consequences.

### Major characters: retain their histories, develop their relationships

**Lira Vashtenri — existing viewpoint.** Her work transformed life far beyond Crowncut, but the current entry gives direct access to her through Cinder Gap. Keep that perspective. Her present project is already concrete: move the accumulated load before removing an old buoy. **Proposed development:** a colleague can execute part of the method correctly without sharing her sensory experience. Lira must decide how to judge that person's competence. The disagreement concerns trust, teaching, and responsibility; the colleague need not turn out to be reckless. Name that person only after the role is established through the treatment review.

**Dern Talish — existing viewpoint.** Publicly dependable, privately dependent on allies who withhold what she wants to know. Her history supplies a sharper conflict than a generic exhausted leader: she inherited a lost home she cannot remember, a neutrality she has defended, and secret cooperation she cannot explain to her own staff. **Proposed development:** give a requested leave, a private friendship, or a delegation of authority durable consequences. She should have something to choose besides working harder. Any revelation about her elven contact stays in DM material.

**Teren Voss — existing specialist.** He both participated in the failed project and preserved the evidence against it. His repair bench is a place where a historically consequential person can still do ordinary useful work. **Proposed development:** bring him a communication design that addresses a real excluded community without recreating his guide chain. His participation, refusal, or limited advice should depend on the actual design and his commitments, not on a scripted redemption. His unusually long working chronology needs a deliberate age treatment before further biography.

**Oram Sells — existing deceased leader.** Keep him present through the practices and relationships he left, with different people judging their effects. His recovery ledgers matter because families can ask what was recovered and what was refused. They also consume time crews might spend elsewhere. Do not resurrect him or turn every consequential predecessor into a titan by declaration.

**Iro Senn — existing station lead.** His friendship with Dern and his responsibility for Gray Line place him between personal trust and command. His unsoftened reports are a continuing choice. **Proposed development:** let him disagree with a Council priority while continuing to carry it out, or successfully secure a change through evidence from his station. His private meals with Dern also warrant reconciliation with the absolute language in her public profile.

### What remains open

Lira's method can succeed. A recovery can end with a returned possession. Teren can help with a bounded design and still reject a second Span. Dern can alter how she leads without resigning or betraying anyone. Containment remains dangerous, and the hidden war remains real, but neither dictates the ending of those stories.

No new mastermind, founding disaster, or replacement cast is proposed for Bloom. Its principal foundation work is to connect already-authored later history, deepen present relationships, and resolve the source conflicts listed below.

## 2. Planetary conflict: Istrava and the Red Sovereign

### Canonical ground

[Korvath](../world/cosmology/korvath.rb) has several continents, old port cities, provincial institutions, and regional cultures. Coremark's concealed northern network does not govern the planet. [Ashvane](../world/cosmology/ashvane.rb) has independent settlements, surface transport, and local industrial and agricultural capacity. These are societies able to act from their own territory.

The [Bitter Reach](../world/history/conflicts/the_bitter_reach.rb) ends in 2423 through linked settlements. Its ship combat remains limited. The [Accord](../world/npcs/factions/tempered_accord.rb) mediates and relies on members; it cannot command a collective army. Its present prose explicitly says no crisis has yet exceeded those arrangements. The new confrontation therefore begins in 2435 and requires an explicit update of that present-tense claim. It is not an undisclosed war running throughout the Accord era.

The [Red Sovereign](../world/abilities/mantles/red_sovereign.rb), [Triumphant](../world/abilities/mantles/triumphant.rb), and [Waybearer](../world/abilities/mantles/waybearer.rb) already permit action at the necessary scale. None supplies skill, unlimited force, or effortless control. Their present or most recent bearers are unresolved.

### The history that creates the conflict

**Istrava** is a coastal region on Korvath's middle sea, outside the northern freight economy. Its principal city, **Velisar**, has shipyards, coastal defenses, upland roads, and a hinterland that can feed more than its own population. These additions occupy part of Korvath; they do not replace its regional variety.

Sava Neraleth brings several Istravan cities into a defense league in 2398. Its captains can escort one another's ships, train together, and keep refuge ports open. It begins with real protection of lives and trade. Her authority comes from maintaining that cooperation, including the willingness to leave a member's local government alone.

During the Bitter Reach, Istrava protects coastal approaches and accepts stranded households from several sides. In 2418, Veska Ral uses the Triumphant to hold Velisar's embarkation ground until an evacuation finishes. The ships leave. Veska dies from the damage the working gathers into her. The refuge fleet reaches Ashvane, where people from **Sereva**, an association of highland towns, shelter them. These become family and military relationships that survive the war.

Aren Talivar commands part of the evacuation and later negotiates the league's demobilization. After Sava's death, member councils appoint him its commander. Some want stronger protection against foreign pressure; others want to spend less on ships. Aren comes to regard their freedom to withdraw as the weakness that will eventually destroy everything the league built.

In 2435 a confrontation between league crews nearly becomes a massacre. Aren orders Detha, a captain who can refuse him without punishment, to hold fire on people who have just killed her comrades. She obeys freely. The Red Sovereign answers him. The act that qualifies him is an act of restraint; it does not certify what he does with the mantle afterward.

His first coerced commands end the confrontation. He then keeps units under orders after the danger passes. Officers who intended to stand down cannot abandon their posts. When Velisar's council attempts to remove him, he makes its coastal batteries admit his ships and commands its defense to remain in place while his troops seize the city.

The takeover is the first armed crisis of this scale in the Accord era. Aren now controls a major port, its batteries, part of the league's force, and an occupation he must actually supply. Other cities remain independent. His fleet cannot be everywhere, orders must reach subjects, and each new resistance costs him part of the life that made his ambitions meaningful.

### Powers with different aims

**Aren's command and the Istravan League.** Aren wants permanent control of the league's defense and the elimination of rival military authority on its coast. Some officers willingly support him; others obey supernatural commands; some member governments still support the league while opposing his seizure. There is an organization to contest and a region to liberate, not one enchanted crowd that becomes politically identical.

Reception matters in the field. A live order carried by a working channel must be treated as potentially effective; the treatment assumes no immunity from hearing it remotely. Opponents can stop relaying Aren's voice or break a channel, but that does not undo an order already accepted. Recorded playback and the persistence of commands across succession need explicit rule decisions before a campaign outcome depends on them.

**Velisar's resistance.** Its people want their city back and their captured relatives returned. They know the quays, the hill roads, the batteries, and the army's households. Some will accept help from any enemy of Aren. Others will not let an outside force exchange his occupation for its own. Their ability to hide people and deny local cooperation matters alongside weapons.

**Sereva's expedition.** The Ashvane towns can contribute crews, field repair, surface vehicles, and military volunteers. Their connection to Velisar comes from the evacuation and the lives built afterward. They want to protect their own families, recover captives, and prevent Aren from gaining an outer-world base. Their commanders will not promise to occupy a Korvathi coast indefinitely.

**Independent Istravan cities.** These can deny Aren a regional victory or bargain separately with him. A city that shelters resistance may still refuse a united army under Serevan command. Rivalries predate the takeover. They also offer other fronts and local campaigns without introducing another identical sovereign.

The Accord can provide recognition, mediation, and channels between members. Actual protection, transport, and fighting depend on people who choose to undertake them. The new conflict does not turn its secretariat into an army.

### Themes and causal threads

**Protection becomes possession.** Follow Sava's coalition through the evacuation, demobilization, Aren's appointment, and the seizure. The league did protect people; its later capture does not erase that achievement. The question is what a protector believes those achievements entitle them to demand.

**The people inside an enemy force.** Detha escaped a command; others have not. Family connections, willing collaboration, coercion, and opportunities to defect divide the occupation. The mantle supplies obedience, not innocence or guilt for everything someone previously did. Rescues and military action can require different judgments about the same unit.

**An alliance has somewhere else to go.** Sereva's obligations do not end at Velisar. Its crews have homes, current dangers, and ambitions beyond the expedition. A breakthrough on the coast may expose an allied force elsewhere. Cooperation creates effective choices whose costs the allies distribute differently.

**Power changes hands.** A subject who knowingly disobeys an absolute command and reaches Aren can open succession. That creates a concrete avenue for a campaign, but it is not the sole solution or a guaranteed reward for a player. Defeating forces, separating orders from their recipients, holding independent territory, and securing defections can achieve lasting results without obtaining the mantle.

### Major characters

**Sava Neraleth — human, 2356–2428; historical titan.** Her refuge commitments and military agreements join cities that retain different governments. Her refusal to grant the league authority over members' households appears in concrete demobilizations and returned property. Aren commanded under her; several present opponents also owe their careers to her. Her history should show the decisions that survived her, including concessions that left weaker towns exposed. She is not a flawless founder whose imagined wishes settle the present.

**Aren Talivar — human, born 2374; titan and new Red Sovereign.** A successful commander who arranged an evacuation before he ever compelled a soldier. The seizure of Velisar, the reversed batteries, and detained units now carry his reputation farther than his earlier service. He wants an Istravan coast that cannot fragment under pressure. Orders increasingly replace relationships he once maintained. He records his lost memories, but Binding cannot restore why those events mattered to him. His remaining family can know him accurately while finding that he no longer shares the significance of their common past.

**Detha — orc, born 2388; viewpoint.** A league captain who helped Aren prevent the massacre and later refused his absolute order to surrender a retreating crew. She escaped through a disabled battery passage without reaching him, so no succession occurred. People willing to work with her include former captives she previously detained. She wants her remaining crew out, and keeps returning to the question of whether she could reach Aren and end the command at its source. Becoming the next bearer frightens her for reasons beyond personal danger. Her skills, local knowledge, and relationships remain useful whichever choice she makes.

**Ivena Sar — human, born 2398; viewpoint.** A Velisar physician who took over a quay practice after the war. She now moves wounded people through workshops and shore houses while arguing against reprisals on soldiers' families. Her brother remains in an occupied battery; she does not yet know whether his obedience is coerced, willing, or both. She wants his return and the neighborhood's freedom. Her patients include people Detha arrested before the takeover, making their cooperation difficult without making it impossible.

**Hareth — orc, born 2379; major ally.** A Serevan expedition commander whose household sheltered the 2418 arrivals. He learned coastal fighting from Istravan instructors and knows some of the officers now serving Aren. He has promised his towns an expedition with achievable objectives, beginning with recovery of captives and protection of the relief route. He will take risks for Velisar; he will also withdraw crews from an operation he judges incapable of helping it. People who call that abandonment must reckon with the forces he has already brought.

**Nalis Venn — human, born 2382; Waybearer and major ally.** She qualified by trusting a passenger's lived knowledge of a home she had never seen. In 2430 Soma Irel yielded an occupied rescue road to her and remained at the other end while pursuit was unresolved. That transfer did not make every old road available from memory alone. Nalis now wants to get people out of Velisar. A former resident can supply an anchor, but an occupied road also admits pursuit and cannot close while someone remains on it. She refuses to make her rescue practice a permanently available invasion service. Her old rescue remains a source of people and places outside this war.

**Veska Ral — human, 2368–2418; deceased mantle bearer.** The evacuation she completed is a real victory. Her death is not proof that every use of the Triumphant must kill its bearer; its severity followed the particular damage accumulated in that action. Veterans argue over the scale of undertaking someone should accept and whether a commander can ask another person to bear such a cost. No present Triumphant is assigned merely to balance Aren's power.

### Campaigns and open outcomes

An occupied-city campaign can free neighborhoods and batteries. An expedition can recover captives and establish a defensible route. A pursuit can follow a defecting captain across several worlds. A political and military campaign can unite independent cities without handing their forces to one commander. An infiltration can attempt to reach Aren; a separate campaign can prevent him establishing an outer-world base.

These are distinct undertakings within one conflict. A successful evacuation remains successful even if Aren holds the city. A liberated city need not produce his immediate defeat everywhere. Loss of the mantle does not automatically explain what happens to all standing commands: the current source leaves that interaction to resolve before any ending depends on it. No antagonist secretly serves the Bloom's hidden war as a condition of this treatment.

## 3. Lithren: exploring the precursor remains

### Canonical ground

[Lithren](../world/cosmology/lithren.rb) preserves non-elven open-air ruins, worn channels, untranslated marks, and evidence of conditions its current frozen surface cannot support. Three major concentrations are catalogued and a fourth is being surveyed. Smaller sites lie across largely unexplored country: some regularly visited, some privately charted, many unknown or too small to have lasting names. The public entry reports no intact body or recovered written archive. None of those claims establishes pocket countries, a particular species of builder, or a working ancient device.

[Return](../world/encyclopedia/ability/return.rb) makes a surviving complete pattern authoritative over later damage. The [Great Restorer](../world/abilities/mantles/great_restorer.rb) can assemble scattered witnesses into a composite return, including a district. It carries one named loss permanently into the bearer. The [Far Witness](../world/abilities/mantles/far_witness.rb) can follow surviving consequences through history, with reciprocal exposure; it does not supply private motives or a future.

These powers allow major discoveries without new fundamental physics. They also impose limits on what can be recovered. A plan, a confident reconstruction, or a few bones do not by themselves establish enough surviving pattern to recreate an ancient person.

### Open scope and discovery

The number, scale and kinds of ruins remain deliberately unspecified. The three catalogued concentrations and the fourth survey describe current knowledge, not the extent of the precursor legacy. Written sites and Encyclopedia articles supply examples without limiting later inventions to those examples. Itharan pattern keeping is one recovered tradition; it does not define every technology, purpose or achievement left on Lithren.

Lithren's ancient works follow established physics with less extradimensional influence. A new capability needs a coherent mechanism and consequences within those rules. Routes, physical traces and repeatable behavior provide evidence about a place. The breadth of what may be discovered remains open even when a particular machine's operation is definite.

**LLM-led GM use case.** One intended consumer is an LLM-led game engine whose expeditions benefit from discoveries supplied by the GM without requiring players to invent their own surprises. Concrete reusable subjects, observable signs, possible interactions and variations can support that experience. This is a use case for the material, not a world-wide division of creative responsibility. Other games and media determine how participants create and discover their contents.

Develop the Encyclopedia alongside particular sites. Machinery and construction practices can support navigation, experiments and recovered capabilities; materials can give an apparently ordinary cargo a distinctive use; lifeforms can add inhabitants with behaviors to learn. Extend existing subjects before introducing new ones. These directions are neither an exhaustive taxonomy nor a required set for each expedition. Specific local secrets may have definite GM answers while the established civilization-wide mysteries stay open.

### The recovered ancient history

**Ithara** is the modern survey name of one of Lithren's three established site groups and the inhabited frontier settlement beside it. It is the principal expedition hub, with passage, repairs, buyers, meals and recruitment available before the proposed gate-complex return. **Itharan** names its evidenced ancient local tradition, not a species or every civilization that inhabited Lithren. **Othes** and **Sahel** approximate repeated addresses around recurring people in the public Witnessings begun in 2427.

Recovered acts establish a local society exceptionally good at preserving the patterns needed for Return. Builders distributed evidence of a work across its structure, spare parts, models, and deliberately retained fragments. An enemy could destroy a bridge or palace and still leave enough elsewhere for restorers to bring it back. This did not eliminate the need for living practitioners, material, or their costs. It made earlier investment in a particular design extraordinarily durable.

That durability supported gardens, public works, voyages, and cities people loved. It also gave rulers fortifications and arsenals that could survive repeated defeats. The patterns preserved the structures' older purposes and limitations. Restoring a fortress did not decide who ought to hold it.

Teams returned tools, doors, braces, engines, and wall sections separately, assembling larger works through successive operations. Each practitioner carried the ordinary spell's corresponding temporary loss. A single composite return remained exceptional mantle work. The practice improves preservation and coordination; it does not give every ancient builder the Great Restorer's scope.

**Othes** is an ancient authority witnessed directing armed custody of pattern collections, preventing workers from leaving with pieces, and ordering a broken defense returned. These actions establish coercion and military control. The identity of the force that broke the defense, the reach of Othes's authority and the wider causes of war are not recovered.

**Sahel** is witnessed restoring homes, working around guarded collections and later destroying essential witnesses during an evacuation. People crossed the approach while the broken defense remained down. The act and immediate escape are established; the opposing force, Sahel's full motives and the evacuees' eventual destination remain unknown.

Neither person needs to be the inventor of Return or a bearer of a new mantle. Their actions made them consequential. Other cities retained different practices: some preserved several alternatives; some altered structures until the earlier pattern could no longer govern them; some kept former buildings recoverable beside later work. The society had invention, dissension, ordinary pleasure, and change.

Late occupation evidence includes contraction of exposed basin settlements alongside changing conditions. It does not establish the forces responsible for either change. People dismantled and carried selected patterns away, abandoning others. The initial restoration cannot settle where all the emigrants went. Do not assign the Fracture's destruction, the origins of a living species, or Lithren's climate change to this history without separate evidence and an explicit decision. The civilization's earlier conflict and its final departure are distinct historical questions.

### Why people pursue it now

Ithara supports crews pursuing different lives. A prospector wants a find that pays for a ship; a researcher spends grant money on an observation nobody will buy; a family supplies departing crews; people who left a company want to choose their own work. A crew can take passage to a known satellite excavation, search for an unvisited structure, carry samples or help overdue friends. Returning to Ithara brings repairs, buyers, new companions and familiar people whose lives have continued during the journey. Some expeditions use independent camps and bypass the hub altogether.

Protection depends on people with the means and willingness to provide it. Ithara's shipmasters, residents and workshop crews defend their landings and shared spaces; their reach thins beyond the settlement. Companies can seize excavations, robbers can trail returning crews, and a hired thief can simply want a ship. These dangers have independent causes. Friendship, escorts and mutual defense can make travel safer, and successful journeys remain possible.

Wealth inequality is greater than in most established settlements. Owners can reserve ships, replace equipment and wait for a favorable price. Crews with little money rent worn gear, share rooms and sell finds to meet debts. Trade runs are fairly frequent but principally serve factions; residents bargain for spare passage and entrust letters or parcels to particular carriers. No general post line serves Ithara. A ship on the ground does not establish access to its berths or cargo space.

The planet's extreme cold and thin atmosphere make a broken route dangerous even after an immediate opponent is gone. Runs can encounter collapsing structures, deadly traps, damaged ancient works and malfunctioning expedition equipment. An outlaw can be the person with a usable shelter or knowledge of the way out, while needing the crew's cylinders, lifting gear or hands. Cooperation under shared danger has as much narrative weight as predation. Some rescues begin lasting friendships; others leave the original grievance unresolved. Neither outcome requires a concealed betrayal.

A composite restoration is one substantial opportunity within this frontier. It would make part of ancient Ithara physically accessible: a gate complex, adjoining streets, workshops, and surviving machinery. The ground and existing foundations supply material and evidence; its former open-air conditions do not return automatically. Modern pressure enclosures and imported equipment remain necessary. The inhabited hub and its other expeditions continue if this undertaking is delayed or abandoned.

Some recovered works can be useful immediately. A complete instrument can reveal the function of fragments in other collections. A restored workshop may contain tools capable of making something modern builders have only salvaged. An intact vessel assembly can turn a collection of scattered wreckage into a practical expedition goal. Such discoveries need individual mechanisms and evidence when their articles are written; the treatment does not grant every old object an extraordinary power.

Other recoveries change the danger. An old defensive machine may resume the function built into its recovered state. A modern expedition can deliberately reconstruct an arsenal rather than accidentally awaken one. The threat comes from particular choices and particular works. Understanding an object can let people defeat, redirect, or safely use it.

**Eris Talven**, a Great Restorer, helped assemble the witnesses for a composite return elsewhere and received the mantle through that working's disclosed loss. He now intends to attempt Ithara. **Selven Iral**, an expedition leader who helped assemble the fragments, wants a defensible base and exclusive military use of some of its recoveries. **Nereth Valis**, the principal investigator, wants enough access to distinguish what the recovered city actually establishes from what either of them hopes to find. Each can initiate work the others oppose.

### Themes and threads

**What deserves to survive.** Restorers can recover extraordinary achievements and the means of coercion together. A recovery is allowed to be valuable. Choosing to preserve a new life or a deliberate alteration can also be valuable. Neither antiquity nor novelty settles the choice.

**The fortress that stayed broken.** Follow Sahel's work, the evacuation, and the deliberate destruction of particular surviving patterns. Modern crews seeking missing pieces enter sites where an absence was someone's successful action. They can discover that history and still disagree over whether the old purpose governs their own use of the work.

**Making a life beyond authority.** Ithara already supports repeat visits, homes and independent work. People who want freedom can defend a neighbor, refuse an employer or leave for another camp. The same lack of enforcement gives a predator room to act. Local loyalties, dependable companions and competing ambitions shape where a crew can travel and what it can keep. Selven wants the recovered site's protection; Eris wants a larger return; Osen wants his crew able to leave with their share of the discovery. Their projects occupy part of a frontier whose residents have many other concerns.

**Depending on someone dangerous.** Hostile ground can force people to put their lives in the hands of someone they would otherwise avoid. A shared escape changes what they know and owe each other without deciding their next allegiance. Ithara's harsher tone leaves space for kindness with lasting consequences, growing security and people who choose to return for one another.

**The paths taken away.** Removed patterns, former transport works, and displaced collections can lead to other Lithren sites and eventually beyond the planet. An off-world connection requires an actual find, not the assumption that every old object comes from Ithara. Existing outer-route people and veiled objects keep their meanings. Finding the destination of one evacuation can be a campaign with a real answer while other parts of the civilization remain unknown.

### Major characters

**Elian Soreth — human, 2229–2319; Pelhari astronomer.** He separates a predicted outer planet from damaged inherited charts, organizes repeated observations, then backs voyages once renewed travel permits them. His prediction is useful but cannot choose a safe landing. He dies after seeing the landfall results, before permanent settlement.

**Vedra — orc, 2278–2371; captain and settler.** She turns back from an unsafe first approach, lands in 2311, and commits Sevrin to habitation in 2322. Selling fittings and taking lodgers makes staying possible; later prosperity gives her descendants valuable rooms and other residents rent bills. Her welcome and the unequal property she leaves behind are both real.

**Eris Talven — human, born 2381; Great Restorer and viewpoint.** His work assembling Ruvian's witnesses made a composite return possible. His predecessor performed it and disclosed what she had omitted; the mantle then passed to him. She lives aboard the restored vessel, judging its stairs without the depth perception that work took from her. Eris wants to restore a place people can inhabit and study, and believes the surviving state permits it. He will name its omission rather than promise completeness. Nereth helped establish the evidence; Selven assembled much of the material. Eris can refuse their preferred use without pretending his undertaking would be possible alone. His life includes ordinary restoration work that does not require the mantle.

**Nereth Valis — human, born 2391; viewpoint.** She joined the survey as a field apprentice and inherited responsibility for its comparisons in 2424. She wants to understand deliberate changes to the city, including the pieces earlier crews treated as damage. She supported Selven's longer residencies and now opposes his attempt to make the recovered complex a military possession. Her younger sister works on one of his crews and wants the site to succeed as a permanent base. Their disagreement concerns an actual shared future, not simply a scholar defending publication rights.

**Osen — orc, born 2394; viewpoint.** A cutter and expedition guide who found the interrupted patterns in 2433. He can recognize where later stone crosses an earlier repair, reach passages heavy equipment cannot, and organize a crew's retreat. He wants to lead an expedition under his own name rather than remain the person sent ahead of someone else's claim. He has promised places to two people who left Selven's company; obtaining his own vessel would let him keep that promise. His findings matter to the ancient history, but his campaign can concern that voyage and those people.

**Selven Iral — human, born 2373; rival expedition leader.** He kept people working on Lithren when shorter contracts would have taken them home. He also concentrated recovered material and transport in his own undertaking. He wants Ithara's restored gate complex to become a stronghold from which his crews can control access to further recoveries. He will employ armed force and reconstruct dangerous works deliberately. Some employees share that ambition; others want a viable home, an independent voyage, or the pay already owed them. He can lose his monopoly without every expedition he supported being exposed as fraudulent.

**Othes — ancient authority and historical titan.** Witnessed events establish armed custody, coerced workers and the recovery of broken defenses. His power depended on builders and preserved material. The force that broke the defenses and his role in the wider war remain unknown.

**Sahel — ancient builder and historical titan.** Her recoveries returned ordinary places to their inhabitants before her work sustained Othes's defenses. The broken fortress and the surviving departure route record a later refusal. Modern people can encounter consequences of both periods. The treatment does not turn her into an infallible guide to which works should now be restored.

### Campaigns and open outcomes

A crew can build a livelihood through prospecting, paid surveys and carrying people between camps, developing friendships and enemies over repeated visits. A promising satellite find can support a whole expedition. Bandits who steal a cargo can become recurring enemies without knowing anything about its ancient significance. A crew might recover its ship, help a camp resist armed newcomers, search for missing friends or finance an independent base. These pursuits can lead to larger discoveries without having to do so.

Living ground now offers further approaches. Kavren's folds reveal a buried recess while supporting a trade in tested skins; disturbing them changes both the access and the colony. Twelve's movements connect occupied shelters through a passage a rescue cradle cannot use. Hesh can expose a camera route and hide it again. The Vannor Account gives independent crews evidence worth checking beyond the surveyed approaches, without settling what they will find. The Sevrin Pledge can put a rescued outlaw beside former captives, while Iral's First Claim can make borrowed recovery equipment the beginning of a dispute over a useful defense.

A long expedition can follow the deliberate breaks from Ithara to the former fortress and the evacuation ground. A recovery campaign can assemble a useful vessel from genuinely related finds. A struggle around Selven's stronghold can involve rival crews, captive workers, dangerous recoveries, and competing plans for a permanent base. A historical investigation can establish further acts around Othes's collections and Sahel's destruction through surviving evidence, without identifying the missing antagonist of the wider conflict. A later voyage can pursue one demonstrated route taken from Lithren.

No campaign requires restoring the whole planet or resurrecting its population. Nor must all campaigns wait for Eris: excavation, ordinary Return, investigation, travel, and rivalry already offer substantial action. A successful restoration remains available afterward. Defeating a recovered weapon or Selven's occupation can be a durable victory without solving the civilization's complete history.

## 4. Relationships between the areas

Bloom's industrial expansion, catastrophe, containment, and hidden conflict retain their history. Istrava's league begins in coastal defense; its current confrontation arises from Aren's actions. Ithara's discoveries concern a civilization older than those events.

The new areas use existing resonance and mantle rules without requiring the same bearer, faction, or hidden cause. A power associated with the Adversary does not by itself establish that its bearer knows or participates in the elves' hidden war. The Istravan treatment proposes no such knowledge.

Particular intersections remain available. A Serevan household may include someone displaced by the Bloom. An expedition may seek a civilian transport connected to Nalis. Aren or an opponent might eventually seek a recovered Itharan work, but Selven's current project and the ancient history do not depend on that military demand. Neither new locus owns every use of the mantles it develops.

## 6. Source reconciliation

Bloom's fracture beat now distinguishes the 2396–2407 lead-up from the 2407–2423 war. Hinge Six's owning incident dates the occupation to 2362; the Bitter Reach now cites it as a precedent. Reconnection and Cooperation-Fracture retain particular dependencies and distinguish sequences from formal eras.

Lira's Coremark career is consistent in her biography, Builders Gone and the Conclave account. Dern's age derives from her birth year, and her refusal of political meals leaves her private friendship with Iro intact. Oram and Gray Line place the elven briefing in DM prose, matching Dern's existing hidden history.

The Accord and its era now describe the 2435 armed crisis without acquiring a federal army. The Bitter Reach retains its scope and outcome while recording Istrava's coastal participation. Lithren retains three known sites, a separate fourth survey, untranslated inscriptions and no recovered intact body or written archive. Its new public history comes from witnessed surviving objects. Existing veils retain their meanings.

## 7. Supporting work

M2–M5 expand the established foundations into further theaters, recovered works, ordinary participants and material culture. Their selections and remaining scope are in STORY-RANGE-PLAN.md. The starting present contains no scheduled liberation, great return, succession or final ancient revelation.

New work follows the existing power limits and the distinction between a complete surviving pattern and an imagined reconstruction. Extend the lexicon when a new regional or cultural family warrants it. Creative prompts remain optional; unused prompts are not coverage gaps.
