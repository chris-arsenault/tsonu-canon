# SG-1: Research for the Glass Frontier's Major Stories

September 15, 2026. Editorial research and proposed design directions, including potential hidden history. Nothing proposed here establishes canon.

## Finding

SG-1 makes its speculative premises change what people can do. Its enemies possess powers that have already shaped societies. Its allies bring different capabilities, vulnerabilities, and aims. Discoveries open destinations, enable victories, and introduce people whose existence changes the protagonists' understanding of their world. Personal relationships determine what characters will risk with those capabilities.

That combination supplies sustained adventure. A large threat alone does not: an endlessly approaching catastrophe can produce the same defensive mission indefinitely. Nor does a large quantity of lore. The productive feature is a world that changes when someone succeeds, with several actors able to initiate the next consequential action.

The earlier Avrel and connected-view treatments fall short of this brief. They develop civic disagreement and archaeological interpretation, but those subjects do not supply the two major adventure settings requested. Their scope should not constrain the replacements.

## Research basis

The episode accounts below come from Richard Dean Anderson's official website, supplemented by the *Death Knell* transcript at GateWorld. These are episode guides and a transcript, not a claim to have rewatched the series. The conclusions about story construction are my analysis. Joseph Mallozzi's commentary is identified separately from that analysis and from comments left by his readers.

### War grows from different powers and different costs

**Summit.** The deaths of powerful Goa'uld change the balance among their rivals. Daniel's infiltration intersects a summit, a planned assassination, an attack on the Tok'ra, and his personal connection to Sarah, the host of Osiris. Several parties are taking action at once; the heroes enter a moving conflict. A previous victory supplies new opportunities and dangers. [Episode account](https://www.rdanderson.com/stargate/episodes/episodes/05-15summit.htm).

**The Warrior.** K'tano attracts Jaffa resistance through military success and personal authority. Teal'c and O'Neill disagree over his methods; his apparent liberation movement is ultimately exposed as Imhotep's bid for power. The immediate adventure tests whom Teal'c will follow and what liberation means beyond defeating a ruler. [Episode account](https://www.rdanderson.com/stargate/episodes/episodes/05-18warrior.htm).

The transferable idea is not that every promising ally should be secretly evil. It is that a movement has a history, competing leaders, methods it can reject, and people with agency inside it. Liberation can succeed while leaving substantial disagreement about the next war.

**Death Knell.** The Jaffa want action against a ruler killing their people; the Tok'ra protect an infiltration strategy and personnel they cannot readily replace. A joint base is attacked, and Carter's survival depends on bringing an unfinished weapon into the field. The alliance's conflicting priorities coexist with a direct pursuit and rescue. Jacob's loyalties cross the institutional division. [Episode transcript](https://www.gateworld.net/sg1/s7/death-knell/transcript/).

That is substantive alliance conflict. The parties disagree because they can do different things and bear different losses. Negotiation can determine whether a city receives help, whether a valuable agent must act, or whether an ally can survive the next campaign. A meeting becomes one moment in that conflict, not its principal available activity.

### A discovery can alter the basis of domination

**Cure.** Pangar offers a valuable medicine whose manufacture depends on captive symbiotes. The captive queen proves to be Egeria, founder of the Tok'ra. An apparent exchange mission becomes a confrontation with exploitation, dependence, and a living historical figure. [Episode account](https://www.rdanderson.com/stargate/episodes/episodes/06-10cure.htm).

**Tretonin's later use.** Its adaptation for Jaffa makes life without a hosted symbiote possible, while retaining dependence on a continuing supply of the drug. A technical development therefore changes the practical prospects for freedom; it does not itself resolve the political struggle. [Official-site lexicon](https://rdanderson.com/stargate/lexicon/entries/tretonin.htm).

The broader lesson is that an invention or discovery can create a political actor who previously could not survive independently. That creates recruitment, protection, migration, raids, invention, and disagreements over strategy. The science-fiction premise continues to generate actions after its introductory episode.

### Ancient knowledge does something before it explains everything

**The Fifth Race.** An Ancient repository endangers O'Neill's mind while enabling him to build equipment and reach the Asgard beyond the team's previous range. Learning about the Ancients enlarges the world through action and contact. Knowledge is a capability, a bodily risk, and an invitation into a larger history. [Episode account](https://rdanderson.com/stargate/episodes/episodes/02-15fifth.htm).

**The Torment of Tantalus.** A rescue reunites people separated by an abandoned expedition and reveals a meeting place of several ancient civilizations. The failing route home and the threatened site make the attraction of its knowledge immediate. The mystery has a human relationship inside it, alongside evidence of a much larger past. [Episode account](https://rdanderson.com/stargate/episodes/episodes/01-11tantalus.htm).

**Lost City.** Earlier Ancient discoveries become the means of opposing Anubis's attack. The search passes through a ruined world to an outpost on Earth; using its defenses saves the planet and leaves O'Neill in stasis. The discovery answers an urgent question while leaving a larger destination still to find. [Episode account](https://rdanderson.com/stargate/episodes/episodes/07-21lost.htm).

These stories deliver answers and achievements. Mystery survives because the answers disclose more of a substantial world. It does not depend on every recovered text being unreadable, every machine remaining inert, or every witness refusing to explain anything. A useful instrument can remain useful after its mystery is understood.

### Fantastic scale permits intimacy and humor

**Window of Opportunity.** An Ancient machine traps several worlds in repeating time. Its present operator hopes to recover his dead wife. The premise permits repetition, experimentation, and comedy, while the resolution turns on accepting a personal loss. Ancient technology can support a very different kind of story from a military race for a weapon. [Episode account](https://www.rdanderson.com/stargate/episodes/episodes/04-06window.htm).

Discussing how he would introduce a new Stargate series, Joseph Mallozzi describes a new team's discovery of the universe through action, adventure, humor, and exploration, with established characters encountered along the way. This is a proposal for an accessible introduction, not a universal writing formula. It supports introducing the world through experiences and relationships rather than requiring an encyclopedic briefing first. [Mallozzi's commentary](https://josephmallozzi.com/2019/08/03/august-3-2019-my-stargate-way-of-thinking/).

## Implications for this setting

### Hope can carry an adventure of enormous consequence

For the Glass Frontier, hope should include freeing a conquered place, defeating an enemy, saving someone thought lost, forming an alliance that works, and recovering a capability that improves life. Those gains can endure. Antagonists can be cruel, dangerous, and wrong; moral complexity does not require making domination a reasonable difference of opinion.

Friendship, food, art, romance, and competence give people lives worth living within that world. They also support independent local games. Their presence does not require reducing the major conflicts to civic administration. Conversely, a great war need not enter every household story.

### The scale is measured in independent campaigns and meaningful change

A major locus needs enough independent actors, territories, capabilities, historical turns, and unresolved ambitions for different groups to pursue different campaigns there. One group might free a city while another follows an enemy succession struggle and another helps a new ally establish itself. None needs to complete a single prescribed master quest.

The same applies to ancient discovery. An expedition, contact with descendants, the pursuit of a dangerous artifact, and an attempt to restore a vanished landscape should have their own participants and outcomes. They can intersect without becoming successive locks on the same final door.

### Physical limits should create usable opportunities

A limited capability can still be enormous: moving a district, surviving a fleet engagement, reaching an inaccessible country. A useful limit gives people a decision they can understand—what must be brought, what cannot be moved, how an adversary can interfere, or what becomes possible afterward. Detail about calibration or maintenance earns space when it changes that decision.

The setting already permits extraordinary forces. There is no reason to make a new major discovery a small visual effect merely because its mechanism must be intelligible. Lithren's non-elven character also remains important: its builders need not become another branch of the ring's history.

### Borrow dramatic relationships, develop our own substance

Copying false gods, implanted symbiotes, rebel warrior castes, gate teams, and ascension would give Kaleidos a renamed Stargate plot. The useful relationships are broader: an oppressive power with an effective advantage; potential allies whose freedom changes the war; knowledge that enables something previously impossible; and a past with living consequences.

The two directions below propose different physical and political substance. They are examples of whole-locus development, not requirements to impose on every new article. Creative questions remain prompts; there is no minimum number of powers, betrayals, discoveries, or strange effects an entry must contain.
