# Side excursions — preliminary proposals

This file preserves the preliminary descriptions used for discussion and revision.
The [canon map](SIDE-EXCURSIONS-ENCODED.md) links their implemented entries,
settled names and world connections. The Ruby entries own the canonical facts;
the working headings and descriptions below remain the proposal record.

All sixteen proposals are retained separately, including alternatives that overlap.
The descriptions below use an in-universe voice. Research references are collected
at the end of this document, outside the proposed lore.

## Proposed descriptions

### 1. The animal beneath the orchard

A Kaleidos orchard grows above an enormous burrowing animal. Its seasonal
movements divert springs and lift whole rows of trees. The orchard families
recognize its resting grounds by the warmth of the soil and the slow rise and
fall of water in their wells. They lower ladders into the hollows beside its
sleeping body to gather fruit from trees that have sunk below ground.

The animal's circuit passes beneath buried structures. Its tunnels expose
doorways, broken chambers and roots thick with pale fruit; some passages remain
open after it moves on. Explorers mark the firm walls and leave ropes at the
steepest descents. A route beside the animal's flank can close when it rolls
in its sleep.

At its present resting place, a manufactured object protrudes from beneath
the skin. Orchard workers have cleared enough soil to reveal a smooth rim and
regularly spaced fastenings. The animal flinches when metal tools touch it.
The families want the object removed before the wound worsens, but they fear
driving the animal from the springs that water their trees.

### 2. The night everyone comes down

An isolated hab holds an enormous dance in its empty water basin before the
seasonal filling. Weak gravity lets dancers leap between suspended ribbons while
musicians and food sellers occupy the terraces. The celebration lasts several
nights. Different ensembles take the lower floor each evening, and dancers
practice their crossings overhead while the instruments are being tuned.

Masks are common. Some visitors conceal occupations, allegiances or a face that
would attract unwelcome attention elsewhere. Others wear elaborate masks made
solely for the dance. An invitation tied to a ribbon gives a terrace and a time;
its recipient often learns the sender's identity only on arrival.

Experienced partners cross the basin without touching the floor. Musicians
stretch the pauses between phrases while a dancer hangs beyond anyone's reach,
then bring the rhythm back as another catches them. Rival troupes compete for
the longest crossing. Old partners recognize one another by their grip even
when neither has worn the same mask twice.

### 3. A memory from tomorrow

A small pilgrimage site offers a difficult ritual through which some visitors
experience one vivid moment apparently belonging to their future. The experience
has the familiarity of a recollection: a hand already reaching for a latch,
the taste of unfamiliar food, a voice heard from the next room. Some pilgrims
remember a frightening event; others remember an ordinary afternoon.

Earlier pilgrims have subsequently recognized places from their visions. They
disagree about whether travelling there brought the remembered event about.
Other accounts describe encounters that never occurred, including meetings with
people who died before the pilgrim could reach them.

Former pilgrims accompany newcomers along the difficult approach and help them
prepare for the rite. Those frightened by an experience often remain nearby
until a companion arrives. Accounts circulate among travellers: several people
have described the same doorway or stretch of shoreline, each remembering a
different event there. Pilgrims sometimes travel together to find such a place,
carrying sketches made before they met.

### 4. The house inside the storm

Travelers occasionally see a brightly lit house moving through a vast surface
storm. Its windows remain visible after blowing grit has concealed the ground
beneath it. Visitors have returned carrying warm meals and describing rooms
furnished for guests of very different sizes. Others have found the same house
empty, with places laid at the table.

Repeated sightings trace a circuit between sheltered outcrops. The house pauses
at these refuges, sometimes long enough for travellers to reach its steps.
People who have ridden aboard leave directions scratched into the sheltering
rock. Passengers reach distant refuges aboard it while the storm makes the
overland crossing impassable.

The entrance hall and dining rooms agree across visitors' sketches. Beyond them,
most accounts cover only a few passages; the largest doors require several
people to move. Guests leave folded notes beside particular chairs, asking
after companions or requesting passage to a refuge. The house sometimes begins
moving while a meal is still being served. Loose crockery stays on the table,
but an open exterior door admits the full force of the storm.

### 5. The borrowed champion

A provincial fighting festival lends contestants an old ceremonial harness.
Its wearers recover from falls with unusual speed and execute holds they have
never been taught. Several have performed the same unfamiliar salute and
recognized a spectator they had never met. They agree on where that person
stood, but give different descriptions of the face.

The festival includes open bouts, challenges between former champions and
exhibitions in the harness. It passes between willing contestants. Some fighters
seek it for the advantage; others enter unassisted to prove they can beat its
wearer. Former champions teach counters to its characteristic holds, and those
lessons attract students whenever the festival approaches.

Some wearers retain a movement after returning the harness. Others remember
the salute but cannot perform it without the straps against their shoulders.
Contestants question the former champions between bouts, comparing the gestures
they remember. A few search the spectators before entering the ring, hoping
to recognize the person who seemed familiar while they wore it.

### 6. The garden that chooses its visitors

A traveling garden opens its gates at settlements along an established route.
Particular plants lean toward particular visitors, and sometimes the gardeners
offer someone a cutting they insist has been waiting for them. Some specimens
respond to several people in a crowd; others remain motionless through an
entire stop.

Different beds flower at different settlements. Gardeners alter the route to
visit people whose cuttings have matured, taking seeds and grafts back aboard.
Recipients bring their plants to the gates for comparison. Cuttings from the
same parent sometimes grow into markedly different forms despite being kept
in the same room.

The gardeners regard a plant's movement toward someone as recognition. They
watch for it closely and invite the visitor into the bed, even when the plant
is thorned or difficult to reach. Their advice on watering, grafting and
handling is precise. Their explanations of whom a plant recognizes differ
between gardeners. Travellers sometimes accompany the garden for several stops,
waiting to see a particular bed flower or carrying a gift they want to return.

### 7. Someone is answering the abandoned telescope

Visitors to a disused surface observatory receive replies when they aim its
oldest telescope at an apparently empty patch of sky. A light in the eyepiece
answers flashes from a lamp held at the instrument's mouth. Visitors have
developed a shared vocabulary of signals and left it beside the telescope.

The correspondent asks ordinary, curious questions. It remembers previous
conversations and asks after people who have stopped visiting. Some of its
observations have been checked locally: it described a broken stair on the
far side of the observatory hill and a spring hidden beneath an overhang.
Other descriptions match nothing the visitors have found.

People carry lamps, drawings and objects to the dome to test what the
correspondent can perceive. It answers some questions by directing them toward
the hillside; others produce a request to turn or cover part of the instrument.
It has recently asked them to bring the telescope outside. The old mounting
will release it, but the instrument is heavy and the dome's stairs are narrow.
The correspondent insists that a particular part of the sky is hidden by the roof.

### 8. The procession across the lake

During a brief seasonal freeze, large animals cross a lake carrying objects
caught in elaborate growths along their backs. Locals recognize fittings from
missing boats and ornaments from flooded settlements. At the most recent
crossing, one animal carried a bell that people inland reported hearing on
the previous evening.

The animals arrive in several groups from different feeding grounds. Some carry
only weed and stones. Others have grown around objects too deeply embedded to
remove while the animal is moving. Spectators gather on the firmest ice;
collectors follow close enough to inspect each back. Families bring drawings
of possessions lost beneath the lake.

Experienced followers mark crossings and recognize individual animals by their
growths. The procession descends through breaks in the ice beyond the shallows.
Divers following below have found submerged streets and openings through which
the animals pass. An object torn loose from a building may appear on a back
at the next freeze. Families sometimes hire divers after recognizing only
part of a larger possession in the procession.

### 9. The guests you lend a body

A small hab holds a celebration where residents leave clothing, musical
instruments and household objects around an empty dance floor. When the music
begins, visitors assemble bodies from the offerings.

A familiar singer uses a flute for a throat. Dancers recognize old partners
wearing entirely different furniture. People prepare beautiful bodies to attract
particular guests; children assemble ridiculous ones to see who accepts. Several
households hold dances, and guests move between them, borrowing different pieces
as the evening progresses. Ceramic fingers suffice for one instrument; a guest
seeking to play another asks the next host for supple gloves.

The guests remember previous meetings and invite favored companions to accompany
them home. They lead the way through passages that become ordinary cupboards
when the music stops. People who have returned carry souvenirs and give
conflicting accounts of distances and destinations. Some hosts keep the musicians
playing until the last companion comes back. Objects borrowed together sometimes
retain a connection afterward: separated gloves continue holding hands.

One guest has appeared wearing a distinctive helmet lost with a ship elsewhere
in the system. No host offered it. The guest treats it as part of its anatomy
and describes its acquisition in directions beginning “through the underside
of the applause.” It readily offers to show the way, but refuses to remove
the helmet while anyone is watching.

### 10. The constellation inside the lens

An independent instrument workshop in Pelhari has an old telescope that shows
the same constellation regardless of where it points. Its lights have begun
answering deliberate flashes.

Observers initially treated the lights as distant signals. Passing a hand
beside the telescope, however, causes an enormous shadow to cross the
correspondents' sky. Focusing it changes the apparent distance between their
lights. Several correspondents have asked the observers to stop turning the
wheel. Dismantling the instrument reveals ordinary glass.

A second telescope trained through the first occasionally shows streets between
the lights. Different correspondents signal from different streets. They
describe their surroundings, ask to see objects in the workshop and sometimes
interrupt one another's replies. The instrument makers mark positions from
which particular streets are visible and copy the signals beside their sketches.

One correspondent wants to visit. It has sent instructions for arranging
mirrors across the workshop. With the mirrors in place, a figure has been seen
walking across a reflection without crossing the room itself. The correspondent
asks that the path be left clear and a particular mirror kept uncovered.
Workshop visitors have begun bringing objects that they want it to examine;
the instrument makers want a view through the reflected doorway from the
other side.

### 11. The ships that wash ashore twice

On a remote stretch of Kaleidos's coast, translucent replicas of ships
occasionally wash ashore. They are enormous cast skins.

A marine animal grows around submerged structures, filling their passages and
covering their hulls. When it moves on, its shed body retains impressions of
everything it touched: ladders, lettering, the shapes of objects inside locked
compartments. Some casts reproduce vessels whose construction nobody recognizes.

Locals cut useful material from the empty skins. A few keep particularly
beautiful compartments as shelters. Walking through one means climbing through
a pale, flexible impression of a ship's interior, with its original fittings
absent.

Several animals use this coast. Their feeding and resting grounds extend through
openings beneath the seabed. Divers follow them to locate wrecks whose interiors
they have already examined in a cast. Fresh tissue can seal a hatch while
someone is inside, and a feeding animal moves structures too heavy for a diver
to shift. The onset of shedding loosens passages and strips away handholds.

Weather, harvesting and habitation gradually alter the stranded casts. In older
ones, collapsed layers conceal impressions of lettering and objects that were
missed when the skin first came ashore. Collectors compare those impressions
with recovered ship fittings and accounts of lost voyages.

A fresh cast belongs to a recently missing vessel. Someone cut a message into
the growing tissue before it was shed. The message asks searchers to follow
the animal and says that the passengers have deliberately stopped their distress
beacon. The cut letters follow a wall beside the impression of a closed hatch.

### 12. The pilgrimage through another animal's senses

A shrine in a small hab offers a pilgrimage conducted while lying beside a pool.
A resident animal rests against the pilgrim, who begins sharing the senses of
another member of its species somewhere beneath the settlement.

The animal is blind. Through it, pilgrims perceive electrical activity in living
bodies, stresses inside stone and moving water beyond walls. Human faces become
almost indistinguishable; a familiar orchard becomes a forest of branching
pulses. Pilgrims can influence where it goes, but must persuade it through
movements and sensations it understands.

The animals range through different parts of the hab's lower spaces. Experienced
pilgrims recognize particular individuals by their movements and favored feeding
grounds. Companions beside the pool compare a pilgrim's spoken account with
instruments on the surface. Their sketches mark buried watercourses and chambers
that workers have subsequently reached on foot.

Independent pilgrims have encountered the same inhabited chamber below the hab.
Its occupants approach the animal, feed it and fasten small objects to its body.
No surveyed passage reaches that chamber. One animal has returned carrying a
worked metal ornament that a resident above recognizes.

Some pilgrims try to direct an animal back to a particular doorway or presence.
Others let it follow its usual route. Hunger and fear can draw it away from
the pilgrim's intended destination, and a frightened animal may retreat through
water too narrow for a person to follow. Pilgrims have begun fastening small
sealed messages to animals that return to the pool, hoping to reach whoever
feeds them below.

### 13. The race through the broken palace

A ruined pleasure hab hosts a race for small, sealed skiffs. The course passes
through banquet halls, shattered conservatories and the hollow bodies of enormous
statues before emerging into open space. Spectators watch from nearby ships;
competing crews repaint their hulls between heats.

One upcoming meet offers a beautifully restored racing vessel as its prize. The
reigning champion intends to win it and has fitted concealed weapons to their skiff.
Rival crews openly accuse the champion of cheating and examine damaged hulls
for evidence of each new trick. Some are fitting weapons of their own.

A broad ceremonial staircase cuts through the middle of the course. Its
landings leave little space for a skiff to turn. Two racers fit abreast through
the conservatory's central arch only with their steering vanes retracted.
Crews use grapples to tow crippled racers home, and occasionally to pull a
competitor within reach of a boarding attempt.

The final stretch crosses the palace's exterior. Through its broken windows,
a pilot can see the finish across several intervening rooms. Racers have
punched shortcuts through weakened walls, leaving torn edges that catch the
hulls of anyone following too closely.

Meets include sprint heats, longer circuits and paired races through different
wings. Spectators and entrants contribute to the ordinary purses. Crews practice
between meets, testing modifications and sharing recordings of fast runs.
Mechanics paint a new stripe on a hull for every victory; wrecked skiffs hang
beside the approach with their last stripes still visible.

### 14. The hunter beneath the ice

In an ice-covered sea on Kaleidos, a small raider submarine has been ambushing
divers searching a drowned settlement. Its crew takes valuable finds, destroys
competing boats and retreats beneath ice too thick for easy pursuit.

A reward is available for putting it out of action. Its hiding area is known:
a submerged district of towers, enclosed gardens and flooded transit tunnels.
Expeditions enter different buildings from holes cut in the ice. Some crews
carry cutting tools and lifting bags; others arrive with armed submersibles
and listening equipment. Recoverable valuables lie in deep rooms whose doorways
barely admit a diver. Loose masonry moves when a heavy load is pulled free.

The raider outguns the small craft most divers can afford. Its weapons have
little room to turn in the transit tunnels, however, and the hollow towers
conceal vessels that settle inside them. Thin partitions separate some halls.
A craft cutting through one can emerge behind a pursuer or leave a noisy
decoy in the neighboring room.

Damage to the raider's propulsion produces a distinctive vibration when it
accelerates. Survivors have compared recordings of the sound. The crew hunts
near working divers, waiting until valuable finds have been lifted before
moving in. It withdraws to a concealed refuelling berth when its stores run low.

The ice above the district has deep downward ridges. A vessel caught beneath
one loses room to turn or climb; divers have reached trapped hulls from above
while their crews were still trying to reverse clear. The raider's captain
prefers intimidation and a captured cargo to damage that would expose the
submarine during repairs.

### 15. The treasure beneath the giant's hand

An old monument in a Kaleidos settlement depicts an enormous figure holding a
shallow bowl. A recently recovered carving shows that the monument, several
surviving buildings and a buried treasury with several vaults were constructed as
one enormous sundial. Successive alignments indicate entrances to different vaults.

A particular alignment is approaching. Treasure hunters have already arrived,
including a heavily armed crew that prefers following successful rivals to
solving anything themselves.

The giant's hand casts a narrow shadow across markings high on a tower. A
carving at its tip is reachable only from outside the building. Part of the
monument has broken away, but polished metal held at the surviving bracket
can restore the missing reflection. Hunters carry copies of the recovered
carving between rooftops, comparing it with the moving light.

One vault's entrance lies beneath a public bathing pool. Its descending door
opens directly onto stairs, admitting water from above. Other passages join
the stairs farther down, crossing chambers filled with suspended counterweights.
Treasure rests in cradles attached to those weights. Removing a heavy piece
raises one platform and lowers another; water entering the lower chambers
changes the balance again.

The recovered carving depicts worked precious metals, wearable ornaments and
an elaborate weapon laid across its own cradle. Collectors have offered money
for matching pieces. Some hunters sell sketches of routes they have opened,
while others conceal their markings beneath dust. The armed crew watches
entrances from occupied rooms nearby, waiting for someone to emerge carrying
enough to justify an ambush.

### 16. The fortress above the clouds

A successful raider has converted a mountaintop retreat into a private fortress
on Kaleidos. Captured vessels hang beneath its terraces as trophies, each
approached from a separate gallery. Their weapons are removed for display
inside, but their engines remain maintained. The owner takes favorite prizes
hunting and returns with fresh damage and another vessel in tow.

Pleasure craft bring visitors to the upper platforms. The guards watch those
landings closely. Below them, decorative waterfalls descend between terraces,
concealing ledges and handholds on the shaded cliff. Gliders approaching the
roof pass over a garden where hunting animals roam among the planting beds.

Glass-floored galleries allow guards to see intruders from the level below.
Weapons taken from captured vessels line the inner rooms alongside working
equipment and valuables from earlier raids. The launch terrace holds the
fortress's pursuit craft, with a clear drop into cloud-filled ravines.

The newest prize is a fast courier with valuable cargo still aboard. It hangs
intact and fueled, nose-down over the drop. Its suspension clamps are operated
from two separated balconies. A vessel released from that berth falls clear
of the terrace before it has room to level out.

The raider values the collection above the fortress itself. During an attack,
the household pilots prepare the most prized vessels for departure. Guards
sent to protect the galleries leave fewer people watching the cliff approaches.

## Research references — outside the proposed lore

These references record the sources used to develop proposals 9–16. They are
editorial material and are not part of the in-universe descriptions.

- **9. The guests you lend a body:** *Kyousougiga*, episode 5. A communal
  occasion accommodates impossible visitors and departures. The borrowed bodies
  are specific to this proposal.
  [Episode analysis](https://geekorner.wordpress.com/episodic-observations/kyousougiga-capital-craze/kyousougiga-episode-5-notes/)
- **10. The constellation inside the lens:** *Dennō Coil*, “Daichi's First Hair”
  and “The Last Plesiosaur.” Contact involves unfamiliar inhabitants, environments
  and assumptions about scale.
  [Episode descriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Den-noh_Coil_episodes)
- **11. The ships that wash ashore twice:** *Mushishi*, “The Traveling Swamp,”
  and *Space Dandy*, “The Big Fish Is Huge, Baby.” A local expedition reveals
  a lifecycle that changes the apparent nature of a landscape.
  [“The Traveling Swamp”](https://mushishi.fandom.com/wiki/The_Traveling_Swamp),
  [*Space Dandy* episode review](https://theglorioblog.com/2014/08/06/space-dandy-episode-18/)
- **12. The pilgrimage through another animal's senses:** *Mushishi*'s
  relationships between unfamiliar life and established practice, and
  *Mononoke*'s “Nue,” with its investigation through a sensory ceremony.
  [“Nue” story description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mononoke_(TV_series)#Nue)
- **13. The race through the broken palace:** *The Living Daylights*. Vehicle
  improvisation and precarious combat change what participants can stand on,
  hold or drive.
  [Cargo-net fight](https://www.007.com/the-living-daylights/)
- **14. The hunter beneath the ice:** *The Hunt for Red October*. Interpreting
  sound creates an opening during underwater pursuit.
  [Naval Institute synopsis](https://www.usni.org/press/books/hunt-red-october)
- **15. The treasure beneath the giant's hand:** *Angels & Demons*. Physical
  clues lead through monuments and concealed passages under immediate pressure.
  [Official synopsis](https://danbrown.com/books/novels/angels-and-demons/)
- **16. The fortress above the clouds:** *For Your Eyes Only*. A mountaintop
  infiltration and an extravagant enemy residence supply the physical setting;
  the hanging courier serves as the escape vehicle in this proposal.
  [Official film synopsis](https://www.007.com/the-films/for-your-eyes-only/)
