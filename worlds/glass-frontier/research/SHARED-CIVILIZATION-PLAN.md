# Connect the three story loci

## Objective

Make Bloom/Shear, Lithren and Istrava recognizably part of a shared civilization.
Let music, recreation, household relationships, visits, food and craft establish
the main connections. Develop three optional adventures through those contacts.
Each locus must continue to support independent campaigns and ordinary lives.

Sulion plan: `fd31b6ce-7f90-4ca1-8674-be4d91a443f9`.
Phase status belongs to that plan. The proposals below become canon only through
the authoring phases.

## Scope

- Revise roughly 12–16 existing Atlas entries, selected after reading their
  current bodies and neighborhoods. Prefer communities, venues, objects and
  groups as the lasting connections.
- Extend roughly 3–5 relevant Encyclopedia entries. Add a small number of new
  entries when a distinct reusable subject needs an explanation that existing
  entries cannot own. These estimates guide scope, not entity quotas.
- Update the affected histories, threads and public GM notes. Add typed Atlas
  relationships where the authored facts establish them.
- Create no new Atlas entities in this pass. Keep the existing cast and places.
- Preserve existing unrelated changes. Commit and publication are outside this
  plan's scope.

Most of the new connective substance should remain meaningful if all three
adventures are omitted. Shared practices can support pleasure, curiosity,
ambition, friendship and disagreement without acquiring a present emergency.

## 1. Select shared practices and their local expressions

Read candidate Atlas entries and their relevant neighbors. Search the
Encyclopedia, then read possible owners before deciding to extend or create an
article. Check histories and travel conditions before assigning a visit,
apprenticeship or family connection.

Choose a few substantial subjects from these areas:

| Area | Candidate development | Possible existing anchors |
|---|---|---|
| Music and reputation | Modern arrangements of recovered music circulate; audiences recognize performers and disagree about versions. | Sereyat, Ressa Dorr's hall, the Veyr Company, velith, an existing Shear or cordon gathering place |
| Recreation | Tovan travels with visitors and crews; different floors, audiences and available equipment change its local practice. | Tovan, Tovanesh, an existing Pell community or vessel, an Itharan gathering place |
| Household relationships | Former neighbors, relatives and apprentices sustain visits and affection across distance. | Outside, Oskara, Ithara, existing households and working groups |
| Food and gifts | Familiar foods carry household histories and acquire different prices, uses or meanings away from home. | Old proof, salt-eggs, Naloven's orchards and baths, existing kitchens and halls |
| Visiting and craftsmanship | People seek performances, baths, beautiful work or instruction because they enjoy or admire them. | Naloven, House Tereval, Ithara's working collections and makers |

Select combinations that fit particular places. A custom need not appear in all
three loci, and a location need not receive an example from every area. Together,
the selected relationships should make ordinary familiarity possible between
each pair of loci.

Existing reusable subjects have boundaries. Old proof already carries household
inheritance, wedding gifts and reseeding relationships. Salt-eggs have particular
origins and a physical response to kinetic lines. Quiet Shift concerns trade
apprenticeship. Bellward Guests describes hospitality along surface roads toward
Sithari and the Keel. Read and preserve those boundaries; an attractive name
does not justify transplanting the whole practice to another world.

Research a few Magic: The Gathering cards for concrete details of social spaces,
recreation, visiting or taste. Use their images or situations to interrupt
familiar ideas while retaining this world's physics and institutions. Record
the useful influence and source in the relevant entry's editorial log.

Deliverable: a compact selection of shared practices, their owning articles,
local expressions, participating existing entities and dated ties. Identify
which Encyclopedia subjects merit extension or creation before drafting them.

## 2. Develop shared civilian life in Atlas and Encyclopedia

Write each reusable subject alongside its concrete appearances. An Encyclopedia
article owns the practice, material, organism, technology or social role. Atlas
entries own the particular households, invitations, performances, possessions
and relationships through which people encounter it.

Extend velith and tovan where the selected local variations warrant it. Examine
food and household entries before inventing another gift custom. Create an
Encyclopedia entry only when multiple independent examples can exist and its
subject has a distinctive Glass Frontier identity. A single visit or instrument
remains with its Atlas owner.

Give shared practices meaningful local differences: what people admire, who
participates, what a visitor gets wrong, what is expensive, and what people
choose to change. Select the differences that suit the subject; these are
prompts, not required fields for every article. Preserve room for individuals to
disagree with the practices around them.

Keep ordinary relationships active through visits, teaching, gifts, marriages,
correspondence, shared tastes and returning acquaintances. Let people recognize
one another for achievements and interests beyond their local crisis. Allow
friendships and pleasures to succeed without turning every one into a debt,
delivery obligation, concealed threat or rescue request.

Use `encyclopedia_ref` for shared explanations and retain the catalog's required
cues, affordances, variations and availability. Choose context and prevalence
from the actual scope of the subject. Extend the naming lexicon when a new
reusable term needs it. Describe local examples in their own right rather than
copying the general article into several places.

Deliverable: a connected civilian setting that can sustain visits, performances,
friendly rivalry and household encounters without requiring a campaign hook.

## 3. Connect the three optional adventures

Retain all three proposed connections. Develop them through the interests and
relationships established above, while giving each its own activity and stakes.

### Lithren and Istrava: Lethai's work finds another use

Lethai already produces directional concealment pieces, and performers offer
passage for examples. Establish the Veyr Company's interest in a performance
use. Let smugglers, resistance fighters or military buyers recognize another
application. Use existing makers, collections and venues; the technology keeps
its directional limits and vulnerability to damage.

Support approaches from either end: bring a discovery inward, seek its makers,
protect a demonstration, use a piece during an escape, or decline a buyer's
offer. The performance has value even if nobody attempts an operation with it.

### Lithren and Bloom/Shear: a missing setting bar travels

Connect Damarat's missing settings with goods acquired by a Pell member crew.
Specify which piece has traveled and when, preserving the current facts about
the carrier and injured operator at Damarat. Give the current holder a concrete
account of acquiring it and an interest of their own.

Support investigation, negotiation, recovery or pursuit from either community.
A return can help the workshop without settling the entire removal dispute.
Keep the matter particular to the involved crews; membership does not make the
whole Pell Freight Assembly responsible for every member's actions.

### Istrava and Bloom/Shear: hospitality becomes reciprocal

Establish a limited history of Karet households receiving temporary shelter in
Oskara after the 2426 evacuation, consistent with Outside's later permanent
settlement. Give the relationship a continuing civilian life: visits, family
news, invitations or shared household practices.

Outside residents can now offer shelter to people fleeing Istrava. A hunted
deserter, a disputed rescue or an accepted standing command supplies an optional
complication. Preserve the Red Sovereign's existing rules and the Council's
limited role. Personal involvement by residents does not turn the Council into
an Istravan military ally or extend the war across the system.

Deliverable: three distinct opportunities with interests at both ends, several
possible outcomes and worthwhile local resolutions. None requires completing
another locus's main story.

## 4. Integrate histories, threads and public GM notes

Add dates where relationships need historical anchors. Keep brief historical
moments on existing owners; use computed spans when elapsed time matters. Update
the affected threads to recognize shared lives and their present consequences,
without prescribing future outcomes or a single journey through all three loci.

Add typed Atlas relationships only for established facts. Encyclopedia references
and classifications are separate from graph relationships. An expanded practice
can connect the reading experience without manufacturing an Atlas edge.

Write or revise public GM notes to expose recognizable behavior and consequences
grounded in the prose. Preserve the mandatory public-note requirement. Add no
private GM truths, concealed explanations of Lithren's history or fixed answers
to open prophecies.

Retain one primary subject per entry. A practice does not change subject merely
because visitors carry it elsewhere. Reconsider its classification only when
the article's actual scope changes. Shared material can belong to common life
or another existing subject; cross-locus participation does not justify counting
it in multiple loci.

Deliverable: coherent chronology, discoverable relationships and public notes
that include ordinary behavior as well as adventure pressures.

## 5. Review variety and validate the complete pass

Read every changed Atlas and Encyclopedia entry through its rendered view.
Review the selected relationships from both ends and ask:

- Can people from these places recognize something familiar and enjoy a visit
  before a job or danger appears?
- Do the local variations make the places more distinct while explaining their
  shared practices?
- Are most civilian ties still worth writing if the optional adventures vanish?
- Do the three adventures support different activities and voluntary changes
  of destination?
- Can each locus still sustain an independent campaign?

Preserve the setting's boundaries: Lithren's hostile physical frontier, unequal
access to travel, lack of a general post line and open ancient mysteries;
Istrava's regional stakes within a mostly peaceful system; and Bloom/Shear's own
history, ecological dangers and containment work. Shared tastes do not imply
universal customs, uniform wealth, constant travel or instant communication.

Run:

```sh
make check WORLD=glass-frontier
make gm-notes WORLD=glass-frontier
make subjects WORLD=glass-frontier
make web WORLD=glass-frontier
ruby lorecraft/bin/lorecraft focus --world glass-frontier --format json
make check-all
git diff --check
```

Compare findings with the starting baseline and separate unrelated findings.
Graph connectivity supports the review but does not establish shared culture or
campaign variety by itself.

Deliver one consolidated review organized around the shared practices and the
three optional adventures, with links to the main reading paths, entity-count
changes and validation results. Keep detailed per-entry review questions on
the entries that own them.
